# Figure captions — macro dual-isolation manuscript v1.89

## Figure 1. Geographic breadth of the global mammal source-network increment

Equal-block mean held-out log-loss difference between candidate C and reference R3 across the 12 frozen mammal bioregions. Negative C−R3 values favour C, which adds graph-path occupied-source continuity beyond R3. The vertical solid line marks zero and the dashed line marks the frozen global equal-block estimate (C−R3 = −0.001814). Ten of 12 bioregions had negative mean effects. The global mammal analysis is explicitly nonconfirmatory exploratory evidence.

## Figure 2. External isolation attenuates the graph-path source-network increment

Frozen held-out block scores (168 bioregion × 10-degree blocks) plotted against block-mean standardized current external isolation. Negative C−R3 values favour graph-path source continuity. The fitted line is a descriptive least-squares visual guide, not an inferential model. Spearman rho between current isolation and C−R3 was +0.287 across blocks and +0.228 after within-bioregion centering, indicating a weaker C advantage at greater external isolation. A separate frozen-output audit showed that current isolation and graph-source-empty fraction were nearly uncorrelated (rho = +0.047) and that controlling graph-source emptiness left the isolation–C−R3 association essentially unchanged (partial rho = +0.286).

## Figure 3. Broadly distributed mammal species receive weaker graph-topology gains

Species-level mean held-out C−R3 plotted against pilot occupancy prevalence for the 79 frozen focal mammal species. Negative values favour C. Connected points show means of the four predeclared rank-defined occupancy groups and are descriptive summaries rather than a fitted nonlinear model. Pilot occupancy prevalence was positively associated with species-level C−R3 (Spearman rho = +0.276), so the incremental graph-path gain weakened among broader-distributed species. No formal unimodal relationship is claimed.

## Supplementary Figure S1. Endpoint-quality attrition in the GIFT plant continuation

Disposition of the 404 frozen GIFT confirmatory entities under the single endpoint-availability rule used for the separately labelled nonconfirmatory plant continuation. Of 404 entities, 118 retained at least one available checklist and accepted high-confidence native rows, 277 had available checklist rows but zero accepted high-confidence native rows, and nine had no available frozen checklist. Only 18 of 59 frozen confirmatory archipelagos retained at least one entity. The plant continuation is therefore descriptive cross-taxon context, not a representative global plant test or replication.

## Supplementary Figure S2. Prediction behaviour of graph-path source topology

**A**, C−R3 log-loss difference under the frozen natural-prevalence primary, on true-absence cells, on realized-presence cells, and under the post-hoc class-balanced equal-block diagnostic. Negative values favour C. The natural-prevalence gain is driven by improved true-absence prediction; realized-presence loss and the class-balanced diagnostic favour R3. **B**, mean C−R3 in graph-source-empty and graph-source-nonempty cells. The substantially stronger improvement in nonempty cells shows that the empty-source sentinel does not generate the primary gain. **C**, frozen discrimination diagnostics. C increases ROC-AUC (0.9095 to 0.9185) while average precision decreases slightly (0.3561 to 0.3502), consistent with asymmetric occupancy-constraint information rather than improved rare-presence retrieval. All panels are post-hoc nonrescuing diagnostics.
