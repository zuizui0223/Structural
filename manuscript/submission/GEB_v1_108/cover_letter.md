# Cover letter — Global Ecology and Biogeography v1.108

Dear Editors of *Global Ecology and Biogeography*,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Island isolation is not one-dimensional: source-network information differs across species occupancy regimes.”**

The manuscript asks whether the topology of islands actually occupied by a focal species contains occurrence information beyond conventional external isolation, generic island-network structure, species occupancy breadth, regional prevalence, and direct or diffuse proximity to occupied sources.

Across 5,401 islands, an exploratory layer of 79 mammal species showed a broad natural-prevalence gain from graph-path source continuity: block-weighted held-out log loss fell by 3.77%, with the same direction across most spatial blocks, bioregions and species. Importantly, we did not stop at that positive result.

The original held-out workflow had deliberately decoded only those 79 species. We therefore preregistered a **disjoint second layer of 96 rarer species**, defined solely from pilot data (5–12 pilot presences), while their 396,096 held-out island × species values were still unread. Before opening those values, we froze R3, the actual-graph candidate, and 20 degree- and edge-length-matched rewired-graph prediction surfaces.

The sealed second layer **did not replicate the overall gain** (C−R3 = −0.000442; 95% interval −0.001098 to +0.000204). Its prediction asymmetry also reversed: presences improved strongly while absences did not. Finally, the actual graph was indistinguishable from the matched rewired nulls. This prospective non-replication turns the paper from a generic “connectivity helps” result into a boundary-condition result: the predictive role of occupied source networks changes with species occupancy regime.

We believe this fits GEB particularly well because the contribution is conceptual and macroecological rather than taxon-specific. The paper separates external island isolation, generic network structure, occupied-source proximity and realized source-network topology, then shows—using both exploratory and prospectively sealed response layers—where the additional network information does and does not generalize.

We have kept the evidentiary hierarchy explicit throughout. The 79-species global signal is labelled nonconfirmatory exploratory; the 96-species layer is a preregistered species-wise validation within the same geographic system, not geographically independent replication. A valid fresh local boreal test was non-support, and a global plant confirmatory attempt terminated without an ecological score. None is used to manufacture confirmation.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. Data are publicly archived, and a neutralized reproducibility bundle containing frozen analysis surfaces and code is prepared for double-anonymous review.
