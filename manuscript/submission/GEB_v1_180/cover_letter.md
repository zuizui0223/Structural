# Cover letter — Global Ecology and Biogeography v1.180

Dear Editors of *Global Ecology and Biogeography*,

Please consider our Research Article, **“When do source islands matter? Occupancy state changes what connectivity predicts.”**

**Why this paper should interest GEB readers (under 250 words).** Connectivity is often treated as if its ecological meaning were stable: more access to occupied source patches should generally imply greater occurrence opportunity. Our results show a more surprising occupancy-state dependence. Across the same 5,401-island geography and the same source-aware reference architecture, graph-path source information changed **what it predicted**. In an exploratory higher-occupancy mammal layer, adding source-network information improved absences but worsened realized presences. We preregistered that class-specific constraint signature in a disjoint 5–12-presence layer; it failed in the opposite direction, with strong presence improvement and slight absence degradation. That unexpected reversal generated a sealed 1–4-presence hypothesis. In 529 previously unread species, presence C−R3 was −0.596 (95% interval −0.744 to −0.464), and the observed island adjacency outperformed all 20 degree- and edge-length-matched null topologies. Thus source information did not merely become stronger as occupancy declined: it shifted from constraint-like to opportunity-like, and only at the sparse extreme did exact topology become uniquely informative. A response-free finite-source identity explains why scarcity exposes configuration, but not why the predictive role itself reverses.

We then tested a stronger conservation inference independently. In a three-wave Azores arthropod system, lost-source leverage did not improve held-out prediction of later contraction (C−R2 = +0.000674; 95% interval −0.02130 to +0.02558). We retain this non-support as a boundary between static occurrence information and temporal demographic consequence.

The manuscript does not infer realized dispersal, rescue or a management-priority score.
