# Cover letter — Global Ecology and Biogeography v1.92

Dear Editors of *Global Ecology and Biogeography*,

Please consider our Research Article, **“Island isolation is not one-dimensional: source-network information is widespread but asymmetric.”**

Island biogeography has long treated isolation as central, and recent work has shown that mainland distance, stepping stones and generic network position describe distinct dimensions of island isolation. Our manuscript asks a narrower question that remains unresolved: **does the topology of the focal species' actually occupied insular source network contain information after conventional island state, species occupancy breadth, regional prevalence and direct/diffuse source proximity are already represented?**

We address this question across **5,401 islands, 79 mammal species and 12 bioregions**, using 1,275 pilot islands and 4,126 spatially held-out islands in 168 validation blocks. Adding graph-path occupied-source continuity to a deliberately strong reference reduced block-weighted held-out log loss by **3.77%**. The direction was broad across space and species, but the ecological result was not a generic connectivity benefit. The increment weakened with greater external isolation and broader species occupancy, and prediction-behaviour diagnostics showed that the natural-prevalence gain was driven mainly by better prediction of true absences rather than improved prediction of realized presences. We therefore frame source-network topology as **asymmetric occupancy-constraint information** and propose a falsifiable source-network contrast-window hypothesis.

We believe the manuscript fits GEB because its contribution is a broad-scale conceptual result in macroecology and island biogeography rather than a taxon-specific natural-history pattern. The analysis spans thousands of islands and multiple bioregions, and the manuscript explicitly distinguishes the new conditional source-topology question from established work on multidimensional island isolation, species pools, stepping stones and habitat-network models.

We have kept the evidentiary boundary unusually explicit. The global mammal analysis is labelled **nonconfirmatory exploratory** throughout. A valid fresh local boreal test was non-support, and a pristine global plant confirmatory attempt terminated without an ecological score; neither is used to manufacture cross-taxon confirmation. The manuscript therefore presents the mammal result as a broad, testable macroecological pattern rather than as settled generality.

The manuscript is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors will approve the submitted version, author order, declarations and conflicts before submission. Data are publicly available from Dryad and the audited island reference source; code and frozen provenance will be supplied in a stable anonymized review archive and permanently archived.
