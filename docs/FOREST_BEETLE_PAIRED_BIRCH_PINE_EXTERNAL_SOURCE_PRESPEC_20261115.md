## Priority new source — Swedish paired birch/pine tree trap experiment (stronger matched design)
Official author [Dryad DOI 10.5061/dryad.gqnk98t49](https://doi.org/10.5061/dryad.gqnk98t49), Frostgård et al. 2026, released 2026-08-05. Published author design **30 physical plots, two birches and two pines per plot (120 trap trees)** in three Swedish burned stands, burn-year groups 2022/2023/2024, beetles trapped using the same trunk-attached transparent interception trap design in **2024**, collection occasions pooled per unique tree trap. Published original files precisely `ModelData.csv` (plot/physical tree identifiers `Plot_ID`, `ID`, `Plot_tree_id`, `TreeSpecies`, `Year`, `Vitality`, `flame_height_m`, diameter etc.), `Speciesdata.csv` (`ID`, `Plot_ID`, taxonomic species and abundance records) and `README.md`. Source published species-level taxonomy. Author paper **already tested** host-tree differences in richness, abundance, species composition, tree mortality and fire severity: repeating birch>pine richness is PRIOR ART, not a new Structural discovery.

**Separate potential NEW test**, only if original site-level source metadata + tree-trap and guild traits are genuinely joinable, is whether pine-vs-birch **taxonomic** species replacement preserves vs shifts the broader host-affiliation functional channel relative to a source-conditional species-to-host trait null. Do NOT inspect that biological response or select subsets until source identity and suitable independent host-affinity trait annotations are verified. Even a positive would remain one 2024 Swedish observational burned-forest experiment with 30 matched plots nested in **3 stands** (not 30 independent forests/biomes or a randomized host transplant).

## Secondary multi-context source — 99 European original plots, only for context-specific tests
Cours et al. 2026, *Journal of Applied Ecology* [DOI 10.1111/1365-2664.70483](https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2664.70483); original public Zenodo [record 17927769](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17927769) includes author analysis `Analysis_Cascade_Decline_Salvage.Rmd` and `Decline_Data.RData` according to the record. Author methods: original **56 fir plots** (French Pyrenees), **28 spruce plots** (Bavaria), **15 oak plots** (Loire), **99 total plot observations**; source contexts differ in original sampling year and effort. Fir and spruce used flight interception traps ~1.5 m, whereas oak captures at ~15 m canopy with different/baited trap design. Forest type and vertical height/method are confounded. Trees and deadwood/decay/TreM were inventoried in field; authors already published full SEMs of disturbance/salvage/tree microhabitats/functional/taxonomic beetle effects. Thus **do not pool 99 as a single controlled gradient nor interpret oak-canopy vs fir/spruce ground difference as a novel vertical niche result**.

The original Zenodo public page presently labels rights **Copyright / All rights reserved**.
