# Spatial hydra prior art — Fox et al. (2017)

Source DOI: 10.5061/dryad.2rs23; article DOI: 10.1038/s41559-017-0271-y.

**Non-obvious ecological counterexample.** Fox et al. already showed experimentally that local extinction can increase whole-metapopulation persistence when excessive dispersal synchronizes population cycles. A forced local extinction can decrease viable donors immediately yet, by interrupting phase synchrony, lower the chance that all donors crash simultaneously. This spatial hydra effect is the original authors' published discovery, NOT a new Structural result.

**Why this matters to Structural.** The Helgeland v1.245 seven-year marked-source time-alignment audit did not support a new annual donor-cofailure mechanism: 2,942 of 5,040 altered year pairings predicted observed future migrant destinations better than correct alignment; 2019 and 2020 and Sleneset dominated the original pooled improvement. Thus a generic positive or negative monotone "more sources -> more persistence" story is not supported. The next genuinely discriminating ecological question must compare two competing effects of experimentally manipulated local loss: immediate source depletion versus delayed desynchronization, conditional on independently measured movement and prey cycles.
