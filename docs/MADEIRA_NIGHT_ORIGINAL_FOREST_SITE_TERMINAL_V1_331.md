## Why the strictly independent original habitat response could not be measured

The original v1.1 author site keys resolved an apparent **47 vs 48** published site-count discrepancy in favour of **47** independent site identifiers. The original source sites were: **5 Laurissilva/native forest**, **13 mixed forest**, **5 exotic woodland**, **24 other (coastal/agricultural etc.)**. Of 134 original Events, **67 were UV-light** and **67 wine-rope bait**; trapping types were not pooled.

The response-blind protocol required **at least two independent native forest and two mixed/exotic forest sites in at least TWO original calendar year-quarter strata**, using **the same UV-light trapping method**:

| Original campaign period | Native forest UV sites | Mixed/exotic forest UV sites | Eligible? |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **2022 Q3** | 5 | 17 | yes |
| **2023 Q2** | **1** | 6 | **NO** |

Only **one** year-quarter met the frozen minimum, so the full cross-archipelago habitat association target was **TERMINAL_MADEIRA_NO_EXACT_SAME_SEASON_UV_FOREST_PLOT_FRAME**. Exactly 22 UV Event rows would have belonged to the first matched stratum, but the source 2023 imbalance prohibits claiming independent seasonal/habitat replication. We do **not** reopen the original species/origin values to conduct a more favourable single-season or pooled-method alternative.

## Scientific implication

The original Madeira inventory is valuable, but its **source sampling design cannot provide the requested two-season native-versus-altered matched-plot validation**. Therefore, the original three-island Azores SLAM observation (endemic +2.335 detected taxa/Event in native forest, introduced −2.515, non-endemic native −0.852) remains an Azores-restricted observational association. No cross-archipelago positive or negative replication is established from this Madeira dataset.
