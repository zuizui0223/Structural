# Structural exploratory ecology v0.1

## Status

**Post-outcome ecological hypothesis generation only.**

Nothing in this document changes the frozen Structural paper, its denominator, its primary A-Islands result, its Tanzania result, or the v0.40 admission freeze.

These analyses use already-frozen authoritative outputs to ask a different question:

> **When does spatial configuration retain ecological information after an explicit island/fragment reference has already represented area, isolation and source context?**

The answer is not “always,” and the heterogeneity itself is ecologically informative.

## 1. Reference absorption: the strongest cross-origin result

The clearest pattern is that ecologically explicit isolation absorbs most of the apparent higher-order configuration signal.

### A-Islands

Across narrow, intermediate and wide-ranging plants, the tier that consistently improved prediction was **R1**:

- climate;
- island area;
- distance to continental Australia;
- nearest outer-training species source.

Mean R1−R0 log-loss increments were:

- narrow: **−0.0128**;
- intermediate: **−0.0161**;
- wide: **−0.0134**.

Negative is favourable.

By contrast, adding diffuse multi-source pressure, generic archipelago context and finally species-conditioned EOG was progressively redundant or adverse, especially in widespread species.

This supports an ecological reading in which much of what initially appears to be a “network effect” is information generated by ordinary island-biogeographic state: **how large the island is, how far it is from the mainland, and where actual species sources are located**.

It does not show that R1 is causal or universally optimal. It shows that these variables absorb held-out information that otherwise appears structural.

### Tanzania

The corresponding fragmentation reference already contains patch area, matrix-aware current flow, their interaction and nearest species source distance.

Adding EOG is adverse in the frozen primary LOSO comparison.

Thus the shared result across the two spatial origins is:

> **Generic configuration is most useful when ecologically explicit isolation is missing from the reference; once island/source state or matrix-aware connectivity is represented, little universal topology bonus remains.**

That is a biological statement about the representation of isolation, not a claim that spatial configuration is unimportant.

## 2. Range/source saturation in island plants

The strongest species moderator in A-Islands is distribution breadth.

Across 886 taxa, prevalence is positively associated with C−R3 log-loss:

- Spearman rho = **0.201**, p = **1.7×10⁻⁹**;
- Pearson r = **0.422**, p = **1.6×10⁻³⁹**.

Because negative is favourable, broader distributions make the species-conditioned EOG term increasingly adverse.

The contrast is large:

| occupied islands | taxa | mean C−R3 | favourable taxa |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 10–12 | 226 | +0.00128 | 42.5% |
| 13–18 | 242 | +0.00026 | 43.4% |
| 19–30 | 201 | +0.00278 | 36.3% |
| 31–60 | 129 | +0.00390 | 34.1% |
| 61+ | 88 | **+0.01901** | **26.1%** |

This suggests a **source-saturation hypothesis**:

> As a species occupies more islands, its potential source set becomes dense enough that explicit source pressure and generic archipelago context already encode most of the relevant geometry. A species-conditioned graph then becomes increasingly redundant.

Importantly, Tanzania does **not** reproduce a simple occupancy-breadth effect. Bird occupied-site count is essentially unrelated to species-level EOG increment (Spearman rho ≈ −0.028, p = 0.83).

So range/source saturation is currently an island-specific hypothesis, not a universal law.

## 3. Extreme-isolation threshold

A second pattern is non-monotonic.

For A-Islands, mean C−R3 by mainland-distance quartile is:

- Q1 near: **+0.00449**;
- Q2: **+0.00839**;
- Q3: **+0.00600**;
- Q4 most remote: **−0.00124**.

The effect is strongest among wide-ranging species. Their remote-minus-near difference is **−0.01281** (paired Wilcoxon p ≈ 1.0×10⁻⁷).

This motivates an **extreme-isolation threshold hypothesis**:

> Species-conditioned topology is mostly redundant in source-rich near/intermediate settings, but can become informative once geographic isolation crosses a sufficiently extreme boundary.

Tanzania appears superficially similar. Mean EOG increment falls from **+0.0599** in fragments nearest a large forest to **−0.00114** in the farthest quartile, with a strong species-paired Q4−Q1 difference.

But this is **not an independent confirmation**: every farthest-quartile fragment is in East Usambara. Restricting the comparison within East Usambara removes the detected difference (paired p ≈ 0.28).

Therefore the correct conclusion is:

> **Extreme-isolation threshold is now a fresh prospective hypothesis, not a confirmed cross-system result.**

A new fragmentation panel should freeze an isolation threshold before response access and ensure that the threshold is not confounded with region.

## 4. Configuration behaves more like a constraint than a colonization bonus

The most striking response-side asymmetry occurs in A-Islands.

Across species with both states represented, the species-macro candidate increment is approximately:

- absences: **−0.00080**;
- presences: **+0.10384**.

The paired presence-minus-absence difference is **+0.10464** (p ≈ 9×10⁻²³).

The candidate generally lowers predicted occurrence probability. That gives a tiny gain for true absences while strongly hurting many true presences.

Thus the residual graph term is more consistent with an **exclusion/constraint signal** than with a universal positive colonization signal.

This is a predictive interpretation only. It does not prove that the graph is a demographic barrier.

Tanzania primary LOSO shows a similar local asymmetry:

- absences: +0.00743;
- presences: +0.06732;
- species-macro presence-minus-absence = +0.10672, paired p ≈ 4.2×10⁻⁴.

The inverse-area reference sensitivity gives the same direction.

However, the Tanzania spatial-block sensitivity reverses direction and is uncertain:

- absences: +0.04917;
- presences: −0.03024;
- paired p ≈ 0.105.

So the “constraint” interpretation is strong for A-Islands and appears under Tanzania local LOSO interpolation, but it does **not** transfer cleanly across larger spatial blocks.

## 5. Fragment-specialist birds

In Tanzania LOSO, forest-dependent and understory species are **less adverse**, not positively supported:

- forest dependent mean increment: **+0.0275**;
- non-forest-dependent: **+0.0551**;
- Mann–Whitney p ≈ 0.032;

and

- understory: **+0.0262**;
- non-understory: **+0.0470**;
- p ≈ 0.026.

These differences disappear under spatial blocking.

This suggests that local graph configuration may retain more information for movement-limited forest specialists, but the evidence is not spatially robust enough for a biological claim.

## Ecological synthesis

The strongest interpretation is no longer “network connectivity matters” versus “network connectivity does not matter.”

It is:

> **Spatial separation is an ecological state assembled from area, source configuration, matrix permeability and landscape context. Higher-order topology matters only when those variables leave endpoint-relevant information unrepresented.**

For islands, classical area–isolation–source information captures much of the signal, while topology becomes potentially useful at the extreme tail of remoteness.

For fragmented forest, matrix-aware current flow and patch/source state similarly absorb most generic topology, and remaining EOG effects are strongly scale- and context-dependent.

This leads to a more specific future hypothesis:

> **Topology should be most informative near ecological isolation thresholds, especially where ordinary source-context variables cease to distinguish supported from unsupported sites; its first detectable role may be exclusion rather than positive colonization support.**

## Prospective test generated by this exploration

A future fresh panel should freeze before response access:

1. one continuous isolation variable and one externally defined extreme-isolation threshold;
2. species range/source breadth;
3. presence-versus-absence asymmetry as a secondary diagnostic;
4. the same strong ecological reference before adding topology;
5. a spatial-block transfer test separate from local interpolation.

The primary prospective interaction would be:

`topology increment × extreme isolation`

with no post-outcome threshold search.

This exploratory layer cannot choose only taxa, sites or systems that currently show the favourable subgroup pattern.
