# v1.232 — Is a documented immigrant still observed at the destination one year later?

v1.231 identified 460 natal nest-marked birds observed at a different focal island in the following year, and 1,789 birds observed at their natal island. These published observations are not complete vital-rate histories; most marked nestlings lack known next-year observations. v1.232 asks a narrower postpublication, **retrospective** question: for documented immigrants vs residents observed at t+1, how frequently are those same ringed birds seen at the **same receiving island at t+2**? A change to another island is scored separately. Non-observation is unknown survival/detection/emigration, **not a failed establishment or death**. These categories are not an unbiased survival rate and not proof of reproduction.

First recorded adult island alone never supplies natal origin. Nest stage must yield precisely one natal origin/year. Hold fixed 11 author population islands, historical birth years 1994–2012 (t+2 by 2014), boundary years 2013–2014 separately, recent 2015–2020 (t+2 by 2022), and 2021 birth records right-censored since there is no 2023 data. Report all cohorts and origin islands, no selection after outcome.

The ecological reason is to separate **arrival** from at least another observed year of **destination residence**. An island exporting many transiently detected marked recruits may not contribute equivalent multi-year residents. But survival/fitness cannot be inferred without detection-corrected mark-recapture and confirmed descendants. In particular, Ranke et al. (2021) already documented the movement network, and Hansson Frank et al. (2026) already studied survival and life-history heterogeneity in the same Helgeland system. This source is one archipelago, not cross-system independent confirmation, and no causal rescue or true colonization effect is claimed.
