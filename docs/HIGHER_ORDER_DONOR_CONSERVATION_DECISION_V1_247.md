# v1.247 — Same apparent risk, opposite conservation priorities

This is the decision-theoretic interpretation of the exact v1.246 donor-cutset counterexample, NOT a biological field experiment. There are two receiving islands with equal numbers of potential natal sources and two four-donor availability worlds with identical single-donor viability, all pairwise covariances and complete island-wide viable donor count histograms. Nevertheless recipient A risks losing all donor options with probability 1/16 versus 3/16, while B has the inverse risks.

Assume a conservation manager has an **equal-cost opportunity to give exactly one recipient a new supplemental source** that is independently viable with probability q. This is an explicit *hypothetical* capacity/access intervention; its success, price, actual biological rescue and future reproduction are not empirical facts. When that extra source is viable, it removes the potential all-donors-unavailable event for the chosen recipient, but does not ensure animals move or reproduce.

The expected reduction in that recipient's cutset risk is exactly `q × P(all original local donor sources unavailable)`. For each predeclared q∈{1/4,1/2,1}, the optimal recipient is **B in world s=−1 and A in world s=+1**. At q=1, protecting the right recipient prevents **3/16** expected recipient cutset risk versus **1/16** for the wrong recipient, with *decision regret 1/8*. At q=1/2, the corresponding benefits are 3/32 and 1/32, with regret 1/16. Every marginal/pairwise/global source-count summary available to the manager is **identical across worlds**, so those summary data alone cannot identify which one-island intervention has higher potential benefit.

This shows the conservation decision that is not identified by usual island occupancy/source count or pairwise donor synchrony metrics. It is NOT evidence that supplemental connectivity or extinction intervention improves real population persistence. The 2017 laboratory spatial-hydra effect was already published by Fox et al.; the field butterfly removal and subsequent 2024 genetic confirmation are overlapping research on ONE field experiment and do not provide the missing source cutset probabilities here.

A prospective test needs separately observed viability/supply histories of ALL source populations, higher-order cofailure patterns, independently measured actual source→recipient dispersal, ecological/environmental shared stressors, and repeated next-year recruitment/capture probability at recipient islands. Those units must be prospectively response-sealed and pass Structural v0.31/v0.42 gates before claiming conservation effect.
