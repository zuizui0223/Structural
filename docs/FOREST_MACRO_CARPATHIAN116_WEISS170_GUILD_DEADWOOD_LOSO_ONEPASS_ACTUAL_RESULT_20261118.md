## Interpretation and limits

1. The original Gloor authors already studied beetle assemblage/forest environment associations. This reanalysis asks a narrower incremental **held-out feeding-guild prediction** question; merely finding deadwood associations would not be new.
2. The prior Finnish 142-site positive was for **Host_tree categories** (broadleaf/conifer/both) and different forest predictors; this Carpathian source used Weiss **larval trophic guilds**. They are **NOT identical response ontologies, designs, or a strict statistical replication pair**. It is correct to report different results and **incorrect to pool the coefficients/p-values or claim a formal replication failure**.
3. Author-verified **Gloor underscore-to-space binomial conversion is absent**. The original exact taxon-header admission matched 0/309 and remains HOLD; the 170-species format sensitivity was post-source-header, exploratory and never becomes confirmatory merely because the biological script ran.
4. Original manuscript **303 species** vs 309 raw header-union headings remains unexplained; source trap opportunity and true nondetections, guild representation (two zero categories), source field deadwood units and stand↔mountain range nesting remain partly unverified.
5. One measured deadwood quantity is not host-tree identity, decay stage, vertical resources, climatic refugia or movement accessibility. The result cannot test the proposed complete forest three-dimensional realized-niche mechanism. Lack of prediction improvement in this restricted response does not show deadwood is ecologically irrelevant.
