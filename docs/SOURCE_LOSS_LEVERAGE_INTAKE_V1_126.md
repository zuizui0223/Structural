# Source-loss leverage candidate intake v1.126

## Goal

The next Structural test is not another analysis of the global mammal response.

It requires an **independent three-wave system** capable of ordering:

1. source state;
2. source loss;
3. subsequent contraction.

This intake gate decides that from metadata only.

## Minimum candidate

A candidate must provide at least:

- three ordered occupancy waves;
- stable island/site identifiers;
- stable species identifiers;
- response-independent island geometry;
- enough documentation to distinguish t0, t1 and t2 sampling;
- an occupancy or presence/absence endpoint.

The intended chronology is:

**t0**  
define occupied source islands and freeze leverage.

**t0→t1**  
observe which source population disappears.

**t1→t2**  
observe persistence/loss of other target populations.

A two-wave system is not enough for this hypothesis.

## Why two waves are rejected

With only t0 and t1, “source loss” and “target contraction” occur in the same interval.

That cannot establish even temporal ordering between the proposed exposure and downstream outcome.

Two-wave systems may be useful for other transition questions, but they do not qualify for source-loss leverage.

## What may be inspected at intake

Allowed:

- dataset DOI/catalog metadata;
- sampling years;
- file names and schemas;
- island/site IDs;
- coordinate availability;
- survey design;
- detection/effort documentation;
- taxonomic scope;
- licensing/access conditions.

Not allowed:

- t2 species outcomes;
- effect estimates relating source loss to persistence;
- favourable species subsets;
- connectivity coefficients from the candidate response.

## After qualification

Metadata qualification does not authorize confirmatory analysis.

The sequence remains:

1. freeze exact source files and response domain;
2. freeze response-independent geometry and t0/t1/t2 semantics;
3. freeze disjoint burned-pilot and confirmatory partitions;
4. open the burned pilot only;
5. verify source-loss and target-outcome estimability;
6. freeze leverage operator, reference ladder and scoring;
7. only then open confirmatory t2 outcomes.

## Preferred data characteristics

Especially useful systems have:

- repeated island surveys over >=3 occasions;
- replicated detection histories;
- natural disappearance or local-extinction events among occupied islands;
- many species sharing the same island network;
- stable coordinates and habitat covariates;
- enough archipelagos or spatial blocks for transfer validation.

The ideal evidence would let Structural ask a genuinely conservation-relevant question:

> after one source population disappears, does the loss of a spatially high-leverage source predict more subsequent population loss elsewhere than disappearance of a low-leverage source?
