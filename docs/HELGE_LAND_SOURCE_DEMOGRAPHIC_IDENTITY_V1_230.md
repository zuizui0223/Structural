# Helgeland island demographic source test — v1.230 source-only admission screen

## Why this system is scientifically different from Hopson and Wolfe

In both recent Structural v1.220 and v1.229 experiments, source connections were a geometric or assigned dispersal *potential* and terminal observations could not identify true arrivals from source populations. The 2026 house sparrow Helgeland study follows individually marked birds across multiple islands for 1994–2022; the published individual history explicitly contains `ID`, `Year`, `Island` and observation `stage`. An island-year estimated population table includes observed and detection-corrected abundance. That combination **might** distinguish established immigrants from local recruits and identify demographic contributions **if** first-observed island, actual natal island and subsequent successful recruitment can be separated. It is a sharper ecological endpoint than merely predicting a 0→positive sample after one zero.

**Important publication overlap and prior art.** The same Helgeland house sparrow metapopulation was studied by Ranke et al. (2021; DOI 10.1111/1365-2656.13580) over 1993–2014: 11 islands, 2,192 recruits, 376 identified dispersers, and non-farm/farm habitat-structured island movement. The latest study Hansson Frank et al. (2026; DOI 10.1002/ecy.70356) analyzed density-dependent juvenile/adult survival and reproduction, and explicitly uses 11 islands in 1994–2022. Earlier 2021 and later 2026 findings are NOT independent systems. Distribution through time is already known and public. Do not advertise the effects of inter-island proximity, natal dispersal, or juvenile survival as first discoveries.

The candidate question *not yet established*: **For islands with comparable size and natal productivity, does the number of successfully recruiting emigrants exported, accounting for stage-specific survival and destination conditions, differ so much across island types that demographic source contributions are ranked differently from abundance or proximity?** More strictly: Does a pre-response, lagged estimate of destination-specific viable donor supply improve predictions of *future immigrant recruitment* beyond origin/destination size, connectivity distance and seasonal/annual environment? Immigration into an already occupied island is not 0→1 island colonization. This is a **retrospective hypothesis only**, not a result.

### Four distinct ecological identification requirements

1. **Natal origin.** A bird's first location of observation is not necessarily its birth island. For an adult first marked after birth, unknown natal provenance cannot be guessed. Use known nestling/juvenile marking and check genetic origin separately.
2. **Realized movement.** A known natal island and a known subsequent observed island establish a documented natal-to-recruiting destination among re-sighted individuals, but unknown detection and survival before resighting still introduce selection.
3. **Demographic contribution.** Presence at a destination does not establish reproductive success or demographic rescue. Offspring pedigree/parentage and follow-up survival need an outcome-lagged protocol, and may not be present in the released files.
4. **Ecological denominator.** Eleven islands from one archipelago do NOT offer eleven independent archipelago replications, and published records overlap past work. True 0→1 island occupancy transitions may be rare because most focal islands were continuously occupied. A demographic immigrant contribution endpoint is therefore more plausible than source-to-previously-unoccupied-island colonization, but must be precommitted before any source biological response opening.

No new empirical system is promoted to Structural confirmatory: a truly fresh design still needs a v0.31 disjoint burned pilot + v0.42 quality gate, and independent system-level replication. Even an exploratory direct-movement analysis must separate study overlap, stage-specific natal identity and future target leakage.
