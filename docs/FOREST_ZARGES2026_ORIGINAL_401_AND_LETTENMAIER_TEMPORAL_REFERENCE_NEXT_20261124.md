## Prior art audit and distinct intermediate question
Jacobsen et al. 2015 Ecology and Evolution DOI 10.1002/ece3.1751 already studied early beetles predicting later fungi in 60 aspen trees, two landscapes, with fungal responses after 12 years. Vindstad et al. 2020 Oecologia DOI 10.1007/s00442-020-04750-5 used that same experimental cohort (not independent replication), with beetle years 2002–2005 and fungal survey in 2005 only. Neither can serve as a direct independent prior-fungal-to-next-beetle validation. Generic priority effects are prior art. Do not claim their discovery. Zarges direct time-lagged cross-site fungal increment remains HOLD.

Lettenmaier et al. 2026 JAE DOI 10.1111/1365-2656.70183 (author Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.17337484) report 52 logs (40 beech/fir logs on 30 plots; 12 spruce logs on 12 plots in a second experiment), 35,059 individuals and 297 species over 2013–2024 except 2020, in one national park. Annual 0.35 m2 emergence traps moved along the log; sampling months differ between experiments. Those are paper-reported denominators only; actual plot overlap, lost logs and empty samples must be audited. Published analyses already demonstrate age-dependent community composition and functional diversity; they do not establish transferable same-log prior identity increment. 2013–2019 = 6 adjacent year pairs, 2021–2024 = 3; nominal upper bound 52×9 = 468 log-year transitions, NOT verified matched observations or independent plots. Never bridge missing 2020 as a one-year interval; never interpret unsampled or collapsed wood as a sampled empty.

New intermediate endpoint (NOT fungi evidence): at matched host, experiment-specific exposure age, calendar context and measured current/earlier wood conditions if available, does the same log's previous beetle identity improve prediction of the next year's community beyond earlier observed richness/abundance? Compare a fixed observation-aware reference plus earlier beetle summaries versus addition of earlier beetle identity, with whole physical plot held out and no training target from the same or a later calendar year than the test target. This estimates a predictive legacy under a strict rolling transfer protocol, not a causal priority effect: stable unmeasured wood properties can produce persistence. A positive result is not evidence of fungal mediation or global transfer. A negative result is not evidence that colonization history is irrelevant. Any age-by-legacy pattern requires a separately specified design with enough overlap; do not pick decay thresholds using published/reopened outcomes.
