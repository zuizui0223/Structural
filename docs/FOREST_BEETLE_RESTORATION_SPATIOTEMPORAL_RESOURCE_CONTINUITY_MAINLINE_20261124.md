# Mainline revision: why do some restored forests recover their saproxylic communities, while others do not? (2026-11-24)

## Immediate scientific decision: a community recovery deficit is ALREADY published, not itself novel

Three source-backed outcomes appear discordant:
- **Roth et al.**, *Diversity and Distributions*, DOI 10.1111/ddi.12870, online Dec 2018 (2019 volume), German temperate beech forest: original before/after control-impact design at 69 field plots with surveys 2004/2014, decade landscape-scale wood enrichment and permanently protected reference forests. Authors reported formerly intensively managed forest beetle assemblages *converging toward* protected reserve assemblages after decade-long landscape-wide wood enrichment; wood-inhabiting fungal compositions still differed. ORIGINAL prior art.
- **Larsson Ekström, Hjältén, Löfroth** 2024, *Journal of Applied Ecology* DOI 10.1111/1365-2664.14586; 2021 central Sweden single restoration-programme field sampled 12–21-year-restored, non-restored and deciduous target stands. Restored stands had HIGHER deciduous deadwood than non-restored but LOWER than targets, concentrated in later decay; beetle composition and specialist guilds remained distinct. Thus these data do **not** by themselves establish a historical/colonist assembly debt at equal *current* resource quality. ORIGINAL prior art.
- **Larsson Ekström et al.** 2026, *Restoration Ecology*, DOI 10.1111/rec.70592, first online Oct 28 2026; 2021 central Sweden sampled 67 IBL2 traps, trap-level functional and phylogenetic diversity. Study coauthors, sampling year, treatment C/R/W, field design overlap with Larsson Ekström 2024; until exact original author plot/trap ID equality is confirmed these are **potentially the SAME field cohort, NOT independent two-study replications**. Authors found recovered functional and phylogenetic compositions still differed from target: PRIOR ART.
- **Macdonald et al.** 2026, *Nature Sustainability* DOI 10.1038/s41893-026-01868-x, meta-analysis of 190 source datasets from 87 articles + one unpublished work across boreal Europe/Russia/N America: saproxylic beetles showed no trend to full composition recovery in observed 16 conifer and 29 mixed forest years after clearcut. This is an inference from study-level published species-composition similarity, not measured wood amount/continuity and not an independently matched deadwood restoration comparison. Published Aug 12 correction DOI 10.1038/s41893-026-01926-4 changed authorship copyright terms only; results not reported changed. Metadata Mendeley DOI 10.17632/yt42chsskj.1 consists of anonymized harvested/reference Chao–Jaccard similarities, not site-level species-by-wood records. ORIGINAL prior art.
- **Tranberg et al.** 2025, J Environ Manage DOI 10.1016/j.jenvman.2025.125448: deadwood translocation 0/16/48 object intervention with pre, 1yr and 4yr beetle traps, standing resources; above ~20m3/ha richness increase, predator richness delayed, detected species assemblage composition unchanged. Authors explicitly cautioned transplanted beetles could be imported within moved logs; presence is not evidence of new local colonization. ORIGINAL prior art.

These studies differ in original geographic units, forest types, restoration manipulation, beetle sample method, endpoint (detected richness vs community composition vs FD/PD), and calendar periods, so comparing published p-values or standardized richness as if comparable effect sizes is INVALID. The new idea MUST explain heterogeneity *within compatible field designs* rather than declare an untreated meta-regression of those 5 papers.

## Revised primary novel hypothesis: spatiotemporal resource-channel continuity (H_ST) x source availability

**H_ST:** At the same current amount of standing/lying deadwood and same current type/decay quality, sustained beetle community recovery requires an *accessible and temporally continuous mosaic* of substrates across the landscape. A one-off restoration pulse can increase present volume or species richness without maintaining early-, intermediate-, and late-decay resource channels simultaneously. A broader/staggered programme supplies such resources at different times in linked forest patches; old forest/source refuges can preserve specialist colonists.

Biological prediction A (within specialist guilds): following restoration, relative incidence/richness of original host/late-decay forest specialists is best predicted by independent *past 10–30yr wood-type supply continuity* and independently sampled nearby sources, rather than merely current total deadwood volume. Entire source forest/programme held out.

Biological prediction B (scale switching): historical supply discontinuity has a larger negative effect in patches whose other suitable wood and old-growth refuges are poorly connected; as neighboring source continuity increases, local historic shortage matters less. This is a pre-specified RESOURCE-CONTINUITY × ACCESSIBILITY interaction, not inferred from study identities alone.

Biological prediction C (different restoration trajectories): with matched current wood type/decay volume and detection effort, **species richness** can recover faster than **specialist channel composition**. Prior 2026 work already documents the discordance, so novel evidence requires explaining its variation across independently replicated landscapes with *dated* wood resources and *externally measured* source pools.

**Mechanistic falsification:** If landscape-scale historical wood mosaic and source availability add no held-REGION predictive gain beyond current fine-scale wood, stand age, current plot tree cover, regional climate, sampling effort and method; or if effects disappear on independently validated similar-method traps / repeated detection, H_ST is unsupported. If restored and target plots have no overlapping current wood state, causal/hysteresis effect CANNOT be separately estimated: SOURCE NONOVERLAP, not a biological null. If source pool estimated from heldout test beetles, LEAKAGE, invalid.

## Frozen future-source endpoint and essential original source data
- Unit: forest plot/stand original deployment year within original study/programme/region; original nested traps/wood logs do NOT become independent forests.
- Exposure: pre-restoration forest history and dated deadwood input/retention log by type, tree taxon and decay class, plus current wood inventory (standing/lying, diameter, host and decay), with original sampling area/units.
- Source channel: an independent earlier than target, species-trait/wood-host-affinity survey (method and taxonomic coverage compatible; unknown never zero) and landscape source/refuge spatial coordinates. Do NOT set 100km as beetle dispersal radius just because DATASAPROX supports historical survey points at that distance.
- Response: species-by-sample detection or incidence with original sampling effort and true observed capture zeros, fixed host/decay specialist traits and common family taxonomy. Functional diversity requires source actual trait coverage; no invented biomass from beetle trap counts.
- Primary test: original REGIONS held out, compare fixed baseline current wood amount+stand/environment+trap effort with current wood resource-type composition, then add dated temporal supply×independent source access. One predeclared guild-specific community-composition or vacancy loss. Source-region means determine uncertainty; do not pseudo-replicate trap counts or study times.
- Source study minimum: at least TWO independent compatible multi-forest programmes with multiple regions, and enough actual W/current resource overlap across history classes; no p<0.05 claim from four source studies with exact two-sided signflip minimum p=.125.
- Current admissible result: H_ST is **NOT TESTED**, no new original response coefficient, uncertainty or p-value computed.
