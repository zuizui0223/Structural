## Single new ecological question, explicitly exploratory and prior-art-sensitive

Does an **original NFI candidate plot/year leave a shared arthropod OTU detection fingerprint across the two DIFFERENT sampling methods**, stronger than pairing these Canopy samples with Malaise samples from another candidate plot in the same original NFI region/year? This tests a **within-local-forest cross-method compositional signal beyond a regional/annual sampled species pool**, NOT vertical stratification, tree-resource filtering, dispersal, individual movement, climate refuge, relative abundance, stand causal effect, or global universality. Method-specific detection biases remain intact in the null.

Original DNA reads encode **sequence counts, NOT organism counts**. Taxa below species rank, OTU identity and detection thresholds can affect the result; hence the response is explicitly original **row-OTU binary detected/not detected**, never nominal species richness/biomass or an unverified guild. A positive result would still be an exploratory reanalysis of one UK national survey, not automatically a new ecological mechanism beyond published forest beta/community work.

## Scientific ceiling and no rescue

This is **NOT** a test of whether canopy and subcanopy differ or how many species share habitat vertically; because trap method and source layer are perfectly confounded, it measures whether a plot-linked cross-method detection fingerprint exists over and above a same-region/year sampling reference. Positive does not infer actual individual movement, shared food-web resources, thermal escape, vertical niche causality or forest connectivity; negative does not imply no local structure (trap detectability may erase transfer). Source plotting may include hidden dependencies; 8 region blocks within England are NOT eight independent global forest biomes.
