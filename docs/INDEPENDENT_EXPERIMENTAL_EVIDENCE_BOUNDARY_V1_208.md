Wolfe offers a **real experimental topology manipulation**: 45 factorial landscape/dispersal combinations with 4 independent replicates each (180 independent microcosms), landscape configurations 1, 4 or 6 patches, with total medium fixed at 48 ml and variation in corridor dispersal versus matrix mortality. This improves causal design relative to a purely coordinate-built mammal kNN graph and relative to Laan & Fox's one metapopulation per treatment cell.

Yet the public author CSV and README contain **final day-21 microcosm species densities** and treatments, not source-by-patch repeated temporal trajectories or an observed later 0-to-1 colonization risk set. Its paper already reported predator-specialism and diversity responses to landscape configuration. Therefore it cannot test when three separately functional source populations simultaneously collapse and cause future target extinction or colonization; describing any day-21 community endpoint as a time-ordered source-loss event would be fabricated.

The two laboratory systems therefore support distinct *types* of ecological evidence, not two interchangeable replications:
- Laan & Fox is published longitudinal population dynamics with experimentally set mixing and source-level density in data description, but fixed four-patch mixing topology and inaccessible file body from the exact frozen endpoint.
- Wolfe et al. is accessible, well-replicated geometry/dispersal treatment with only day-21 final abundance in its public source table.

Neither independently validates dispersal edges or occupancy of the mammal island map, and these two experimental datasets must not be pooled to manufacture one causal estimate. This is an evidence-map result, not a biological finding.
