# Macro dual-isolation synthesis v1.82

## Core result

The strongest current ecological synthesis is no longer an **extreme-isolation handoff**. It is a **source-network contrast window**:

> External island isolation and species-conditioned internal source isolation are distinct ecological axes. Internal source-network topology adds nonredundant occurrence information when the occupied insular source network retains structural contrast, but that increment weakens both when external isolation makes source networks sparse and when broad species occupancy makes source networks saturated or homogeneous.

This remains a synthesis of explicitly separated evidence classes, not a new confirmatory result.

## Global mammal macroanalysis

The empirical core is the 5,401-island mammal analysis. After excluding the contaminated 15-island block and 176 islands overlapping the earlier 318-island system, the analysis used 1,275 pilot islands and 4,126 held-out islands across 168 spatial blocks and 12 bioregions. The pilot fixed 79 focal species.

The frozen exploratory primary was:

- C−R3 = **−0.001814**
- 95% block-bootstrap interval = **[−0.002817, −0.000997]**
- **108/168** blocks had C−R3 < 0
- **10/12** bioregions had a negative equal-block mean
- **64/79** focal species had a negative species-level mean C−R3

The result is therefore geographically and taxonomically broad within the mammal system, but it is still nonconfirmatory exploratory evidence.

## Place-level attenuation: external isolation

The frozen post-hoc diagnostics show that block-level C−R3 becomes less negative as current external isolation increases.

- equal-block Spearman rho = **+0.287**
- within-bioregion-centered rho = **+0.228**

Because negative C−R3 favors C, positive rho means the graph-path source-continuity increment weakens as external isolation rises.

The independent 318-island mammal stress test points in the same direction. Its mean C−R3 was **−0.05593** in the extreme-isolation tail versus **−0.10138** outside that tail. The predeclared extreme-minus-non-extreme contrast was **+0.04545**, with 95% CI **[−0.00319, +0.08836]**. That test did not establish a significant reversal, but it independently rejects the original idea that extreme mainland isolation should amplify the source-network increment.

## Species-level attenuation: occupancy breadth

The frozen 79-species diagnostic provides a second attenuation axis.

Pilot occupancy breadth versus species-level mean C−R3 had Spearman rho = **+0.276**. Broadly distributed species therefore tended to receive a weaker incremental benefit from graph-path source continuity.

The rank-defined breadth groups were:

| breadth group | pilot presence | mean C−R3 | median C−R3 | negative species |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Q1 | 13–17 | −0.00483 | −0.00360 | 18/20 |
| Q2 | 17–23 | −0.00562 | −0.00513 | 19/20 |
| Q3 | 24–47 | −0.00275 | −0.00172 | 17/20 |
| Q4 | 57–496 | −0.00146 | −0.00014 | 10/19 |

The result does not prove a unimodal relationship, but it strongly suggests that the broadest species have less topological source information left beyond occupancy breadth, regional prevalence, and direct/diffuse source proximity.

## Source-network contrast window

The place- and species-level diagnostics point to a common interpretation.

At one limit, extreme external isolation can make occupied source networks **too sparse**: few plausible sources remain, graph paths become uniformly long or absent, and topology adds little beyond R3.

At the other limit, broad species occupancy can make source networks **too saturated**: many islands are occupied, graph relationships become more homogeneous, and topology again becomes increasingly redundant with prevalence and Euclidean source context.

The candidate ecological principle is therefore not “source topology matters most at maximum isolation.” It is:

> **Source topology is informative while the occupied source network retains enough heterogeneity to distinguish targets.**

This is frozen only as a future hypothesis. The present data do not identify realised dispersal paths or establish causal depletion/saturation.

## Plant context

The pristine GIFT design contained 503 primary islands, 99 pilot islands, 404 confirmatory islands, 224 pilot-selected species, and 59 confirmatory archipelagos. Its fresh confirmatory lane terminated after response access began because list_ID 180 returned zero species rows. No ecological primary was scored, so the fresh plant system remains neutral for direction.

A separate nonconfirmatory endpoint-available continuation applied one common availability rule to all 596 frozen confirmatory lists. Of 404 frozen confirmatory entities:

- **118** retained at least one available list plus accepted high-confidence native rows;
- **286** were excluded;
- only **9** had no available list at all;
- **277** had available list data but zero accepted high-confidence native rows under the frozen endpoint-quality rule;
- only **18/59** confirmatory archipelagos retained at least one entity.

Within this strongly selected endpoint-available subset, the original frozen predictions gave:

- C−R3 = **−0.04749**
- bootstrap 95% interval = **[−0.11333, −0.00432]**

The sign is concordant with the mammal result, but the endpoint attrition is too severe for a plant generality or replication claim. The correct wording is descriptive cross-taxon concordance only.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. **Global 5,401-island mammals** — empirical core; geographically broad, directionally supportive, explicitly nonconfirmatory exploratory.
2. **318-island mammals** — independent design-frozen nonfresh stress evidence; supports attenuation direction but not the original extreme-isolation amplification hypothesis.
3. **GIFT plants fresh lane** — pristine global attempt, terminal without ecological score; neutral for direction.
4. **GIFT endpoint-available lane** — strongly selected nonconfirmatory subset with the same C−R3 sign; descriptive cross-taxon context only.
5. **Boreal beetles** — valid fresh local non-support; boundary case showing the effect is not universal.
6. **A-Islands/Tanzania** — historical discovery and motivation only.

## Novelty

The contribution is not simply that graphs or multidimensional isolation matter. The sharper gap is whether the **realized occupied source network of a focal species** contains predictive information after generic island isolation, species-by-region prevalence, global occupancy breadth, and direct/diffuse occupied-source proximity have already been represented.

The global mammal analysis indicates that it does. The deeper and more surprising result is that this source-topological information has ecological limits on both sides: it attenuates when target islands become more externally isolated and when species become more broadly distributed.

That yields a falsifiable next-generation prediction: internal source topology should matter most when source networks retain **intermediate structural contrast**, not necessarily at either extreme of sparsity or saturation.

## Claim boundary

Permitted central claim:

> In a global nonconfirmatory mammal macroanalysis, species-conditioned graph-path source continuity retained held-out occurrence information beyond a strong reference containing external isolation, generic island structure, occupancy breadth, regional prevalence, and direct/diffuse occupied-source context. The increment was geographically broad but weakened with both greater external isolation and broader species occupancy. A strongly endpoint-filtered plant subset showed the same sign but cannot be treated as replication.

Do not write “fresh global confirmation,” “two-taxon replication,” “universal source-network contrast window,” “GIFT confirms the mammal result,” or causal source-depletion/source-saturation claims.
