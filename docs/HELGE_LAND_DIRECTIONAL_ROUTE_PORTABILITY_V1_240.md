# v1.240 — Is the DIRECTION of natal dispersal transferable across birth cohorts?

A genuine Structural question, beyond simply counting natal source islands, is **whether stable spatial asymmetry adds information beyond undirected connectivity**. The Helgeland bird records contain 259 documented natal-to-another-island first-year resightings from 1994–2013 births, and 173 from 2015–2021 births. The 2014 bridge cohort (28) is explicitly excluded. These are two disjoint birth cohorts but **not independent replicate archipelagos**; the experiment was already published, and Ranke et al. 2021 studied its older spatial-dispersal structure.

Fixed conditional-on-a-documented-migrant destination prediction, with all origin islands retained:

1. Reference P0: recipient attractiveness measured as total historical *incoming* resight counts (plus one pseudocount for each possible receiving island), excluding self.
2. Reference R1: for natal origin i and receiving island j, undirected historical pair `n(i,j)+n(j,i)`; shrink its distribution to P0 with ten prior pseudocounts. This captures *geographic pairing* and destination demand without asking which direction was taken.
3. Candidate C: directed historical edge `n(i,j)` for each eligible recipient, with ten prior pseudocounts around R1. Only C distinguishes i→j from j→i. Primary signed statistic: **C−R1 mean negative log-likelihood of the observed 173 later migrants' receiving islands**. If negative: a retrospective out-of-time directional prediction benefit; if zero/positive: no such benefit. Secondary: R1−P0. Sensitivities of Dirichlet-pseudocount tau=5 and tau=20 are **predeclared before this version's scoring**, and both reported irrespective of sign. Dataset source, cohort counts, model definitions and signs are frozen in `development/helgeland_directional_routing_temporal_contract_v1_240.json` before the main code was written.

The prediction is conditional on a natal-known marked bird being observed on **another island** as a first-year adult. It does **NOT** predict whether it emigrates, the risk of source-population collapse, or recipient population persistence. The reference controls historical recipient popularity and symmetric pairwise mobility, but lacks independent contemporaneous habitat, geography, detection effort, prey availability and donor reproductive value. It is not GEB R3. Sharing the same source among recipients is an observed dependency only, not proof of synchronous demographic loss.

With no newly accessed response and no chance to retroactively pick a favorable island/tau, this provides a strict *retrospective temporal portability check* for interpreting old natal-source networks. A successful directionality check could motivate independently planned future recipient immigration measurements, but cannot lift original GEB scientific HOLD or convert 2021/2025/2026 Helgeland research into independent confirmation.
