# Forest beetle assembly debt: original Swedish restoration cohort source lineage (24 Nov 2026)

## Scientific novelty reset (before any new source species response)

The raw observation that restoration does not reproduce specialist/functional beetle composition has **already been published** and is not a new H1 effect. The relevant new research question is **WHEN local/landscape restoration recovers specialist/community composition and when it does not**, conditional on currently available wood state, restoration continuity and independently accessible source beetle communities. Cross-source methods and source geographic strata must be aligned before any pooled comparison.

Important contrasting literature, not new Structural findings:
- **Larsson Ekström et al. 2024**, Journal of Applied Ecology DOI 10.1111/1365-2664.14586: central Sweden 2021 beetles compared 12–21-year-restored deciduous stands, nonrestored and target stands. Restored stands had more deciduous deadwood than nonrestored but LESS than targets; restoration did NOT reach target taxonomic guild abundances/species composition.
- **Larsson Ekström et al. 2026**, Restoration Ecology DOI 10.1111/rec.70592, publication 28 Oct 2026: same restoration framework and original 2021 IBL2 flight traps, **67 traps**, including local measured wood at 25m radius and phylogenetic/functional traits. Trait-based restored-community composition did not converge to target. These likely share the same field observations, based on date, site framework, trap and authors; verify exact author sample keys before deciding independent lineage. NEVER count these as independent study programmes without row-ID evidence.
- **Germany 2004/2014 landscape-scale deadwood enrichment**, DOI 10.1111/ddi.12870, 69 original plots, sampling methods include flight traps and direct wood searches. Authors report former intensively managed plots become compositionally similar to reserve plots after a decade. This is not identical to 2021 Sweden: forest type, reference, protocols, composition estimands and restoration footprint differ.
- **Macdonald et al. 2026**, Nature Sustainability DOI 10.1038/s41893-026-01868-x, boreal clearcut long-term evidence: saproxylic assemblages showed no trend of recovery within available 16–29 years; not a matched restoration intervention with known current wood equal.
- **Larsson Ekström et al. 2026 Oikos** DOI 10.1002/oik.12470, Dryad 10.5061/dryad.8cz8w9h6w: separate pine restoration / Effaråsen wood-fungi-beetle substrate trait study, parent trees and years must be audited before using as independent or matching landscape programme.

**Specific trap:** 2024 and 2026 Swedish experiments do not establish that all target and restored forests had equal PRESENT deadwood quality; they actually differ in deadwood volume and decay distribution. An "assembly debt with current resource fully restored" would be unwarranted from their unadjusted contrast. The 2018 German contrast reports community convergence (opposite sign) and provides a falsifying comparator, NOT independent causal proof of landscape network effects.

## H1/H2 revised falsifiers and estimand
Under matched PRESENT wood resources, explicitly dated interruption versus continuous supply should produce an incremental deficit in **functional channel richness/occupancy** only if true historical continuity and independent donor pool establish potential access. At equal observed total richness, trait/guild loss vs richness compensation is a distinct contrast, NOT implied by 2024 author reported alpha difference; original trap-derived count and distinct species guilds are not the same as biomass or functional dispersion. If same-resource overlap is absent, STOP H1 rather than extrapolate a trend. If composition converges in a distinct network with documented historical refugia but fails elsewhere, test restoration footprint/source-pool coverage as predictors, controlling for study method, not a posthoc analogy.
