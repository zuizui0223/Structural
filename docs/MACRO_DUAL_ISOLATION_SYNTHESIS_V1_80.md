# Macro dual-isolation synthesis v1.80

## Core ecological result

The working story is no longer **“extreme mainland isolation triggers a source-pool handoff.”** The current evidence supports a different formulation:

> **Island isolation is not one-dimensional. External separation from major landmasses and internal continuity to the focal species' occupied insular source network are distinct axes; however, the extra information in internal source topology weakens rather than strengthens toward extreme external isolation.**

This is an evidence synthesis, not a new confirmatory result.

## Global mammal macroanalysis

The final global mammal population contains 5,401 islands after removing the contaminated 15-island block and 176 islands overlapping the historical 318-island system. The separately defined nonconfirmatory analysis used 1,275 pilot islands and 4,126 held-out islands, 79 pilot-selected mammal species, and 168 spatial validation blocks.

The frozen exploratory primary was C−R3 = **−0.001814**, with a 95% block-bootstrap interval of **[−0.002817, −0.000997]** across 325,954 held-out island × species cells.

The signal was geographically broad: **108/168 blocks** had C−R3 < 0 and **10/12 bioregions** had a negative equal-block mean. Only Neartic and Oceanina had positive mean effects.

## External isolation attenuates the source-network increment

Post-hoc nonrescuing diagnostics linked each frozen block score to response-independent block-mean island state. For current external isolation, equal-block Spearman rho = **+0.287** and the within-bioregion-centered rho = **+0.228**.

Because negative C−R3 favours the source-network candidate, a positive rho means the candidate advantage becomes **less negative** as external isolation increases.

The independent 318-island mammal stress test points the same way: extreme C−R3 = **−0.05593**, non-extreme = **−0.10138**, and extreme − non-extreme = **+0.04545** with 95% CI **[−0.00319, +0.08836]**. That test did not establish a significant reversal, but its point direction and the global diagnostic both indicate attenuation rather than amplification.

## Revised mechanism hypothesis

A plausible working model is **source-network depletion**. At moderate external isolation, multiple occupied insular sources can still exist and differ in graph-path relationship to a target island. At extreme external isolation, the plausible occupied source pool may become sparse, truncated, or uniformly distant, leaving less source-topology variation for C to explain beyond R3.

This mechanism is not causally established. It is frozen as a future-only hypothesis in `development/prospective_source_network_attenuation_hypothesis_v1_80.json`.

The GIFT system reached a pristine global design with 503 primary islands, 99 pilot islands, 404 confirmatory islands, 224 pilot-selected species, and 59 confirmatory archipelagos. The fresh confirmatory execution terminated after response access began because list_ID 180 returned zero species rows. No ecological C−R3 score was produced, and the fresh lane cannot be rerun or counted as support/non-support.

A separately labelled nonconfirmatory endpoint-availability analysis is queued only to recover descriptive plant direction. It cannot restore fresh status.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. **Global 5,401-island mammals** — large, geographically broad, directionally supportive, but explicitly nonconfirmatory exploratory evidence.
2. **318-island mammals** — independent design-frozen stress evidence; source continuity improves prediction descriptively, while extreme-isolation amplification is unsupported.
3. **GIFT plants** — pristine global attempt, terminal before ecological scoring; neutral for direction.
4. **Boreal beetles** — fresh local test, primary non-support; useful boundary case but not the macro core.
5. **A-Islands/Tanzania** — discovery and historical motivation, not confirmation of the revised hypothesis.

## Novelty

The novelty is not that graphs, stepping stones, or multidimensional isolation matter. The sharper gap is whether the **realized occupied source network of a focal species** contains nonredundant held-out information after generic island isolation, species-by-region structure, occupancy breadth, and direct/diffuse source proximity are represented.

The global mammal analysis says yes descriptively across much of the world. The unexpected result is that this nonredundant source-network information is **weaker**, not stronger, toward extreme external isolation.

## Claim boundary

Permitted wording:

> In a separately defined nonconfirmatory global mammal macroanalysis, species-conditioned graph-path source continuity improved held-out occurrence prediction beyond a strong R3 reference across a majority of spatial blocks and most bioregions. The incremental gain weakened with increasing external isolation, consistent in direction with an earlier independent 318-island mammal stress test.

Do not write “confirmed across taxa,” “fresh global confirmation,” “GIFT replicated the result,” or causal dispersal/source-collapse claims.

**Island isolation is not one-dimensional: source-network information is widespread but attenuates with external isolation**
